## 📚 About the Course

The goal of this course is to help students develop a practical framework for analyzing, managing, and improving the processes that deliver goods and services. Students learn to view organizations as systems of activities, resources, inventories, and decisions that must be coordinated to match supply with demand while balancing cost, quality, speed, flexibility, responsiveness, and resilience.

The course begins with the strategic role of operations management and the process view of organizations. Students learn to map processes and evaluate performance using capacity, flow rate, cycle time, flow time, inventory, utilization, and implied utilization. They apply bottleneck analysis, Little’s Law, labor-cost analysis, and line balancing to identify inefficiencies and compare improvement alternatives.

Students then study quality management, followed by process variability and queueing. Topics include common- and assignable-cause variation, control charts, process capability, arrival and processing-time uncertainty, waiting times, queue lengths, system stability, multiple servers, and queue pooling.

The inventory module examines setup times, batching, and the trade-off between capacity and inventory. Students apply the Economic Order Quantity model, multi-product ordering methods, and the Newsvendor model to decisions under deterministic and uncertain demand. They also evaluate service levels and managerial levers such as improved forecasting, quick response, and postponement.

The course then introduces forecasting methods used in inventory, staffing, capacity, and planning decisions. Students compare naïve, moving-average, and exponential-smoothing methods; assess forecast accuracy using MAE and MSE; and model trend and seasonality. The course concludes with project management, including activity-on-node networks, dependency relationships, critical paths, forward and backward passes, slack, and project completion time.

By the end of the semester, students will be able to:

* Explain how operations management supports organizational strategy and the matching of supply with demand.
* Model manufacturing and service processes using activities, resources, buffers, and flow units.
* Calculate and interpret key measures of process performance, labor utilization, quality, queueing, inventory, forecasting, and project performance.
* Diagnose problems caused by bottlenecks, limited capacity, poor task allocation, setup times, variability, congestion, and quality variation.
* Apply Little’s Law, queueing methods, EOQ, Newsvendor, forecasting, control-chart, and critical-path techniques.
* Evaluate operational trade-offs and compare alternative improvement strategies.
* Translate quantitative analysis into clear managerial recommendations.

## 🎓 Letter Grade Scale

This course uses a **fixed grading scale**. **Grades are not curved.** Your letter grade depends solely on the percentage you earn throughout the semester and is **not** affected by the performance of other students.

| Letter Grade | Final Percentage |
|:-------------|:----------------:|
| **A**  | **93–100** |
| **A−** | **90–92.9** |
| **B+** | **87–89.9** |
| **B**  | **83–86.9** |
| **B−** | **80–82.9** |
| **C+** | **77–79.9** |
| **C**  | **73–76.9** |
| **C−** | **70–72.9** |
| **D+** | **67–69.9** |
| **D**  | **60–66.9** |
| **F**  | **Below 60** |

> ⚠️ Final percentages will be calculated to one decimal place. Grades will not be rounded to the next letter-grade threshold.

**Example**

If a student earns:

- Participation: **95**
- In-Class Group Activities: **90**
- Homework: **88**
- Midterm 1: **82**
- Midterm 2: **86**
- Final Exam: **91**

Then the final percentage is: (95 × 10%) + (90 × 10%) + (88 × 20%) + (82 × 15%) + (86 × 15%) + (91 × 30%) = **88.6%**
The resulting percentage corresponds to a letter grade of **B+**.


> ⚠️ **Make-up exams:** A make-up exam may be granted for a documented medical emergency, university-approved absence, religious conflict, or other unavoidable circumstance approved by the instructor. Students should notify the instructor as early as reasonably possible.
